How much optimizer behavior survives translation into a synthetic workload?

A fragment-level comparison of query optimizer execution plans between an original SQL workload and its synthetic counterpart, using exhaustive enumeration of connected downward execution fragments rather than rooted subtree matching.

Methodology

Why Execution Fragments, Not Rooted Subtrees

This study departs deliberately from the conventional practice of comparing query optimizer plans via rooted subtree isomorphism. The rationale, generation procedure, and metric definition are described below.

Coverage(q)  =  |Fcommon(q)|  /  |Foriginal(q)|   where  Fcommon(q) = Foriginal(q) ∩ Fsynthetic(q)

1. The limitation of rooted subtree matching

Rooted subtree comparison requires an exact match of an entire subtree beneath a chosen anchor node, including every descendant down to the leaves. This all-or-nothing criterion is brittle: a single substituted scan method or an additional join at the fringe of an otherwise identical plan shape causes the whole subtree comparison to fail, even though the optimizer's higher-level structural reasoning — how it orders joins, which access paths it prefers, how it composes aggregation — was faithfully reproduced. Rooted comparison therefore systematically undercounts genuine structural agreement between two plans that differ only in localized, often cosmetically-motivated regions of the tree.

2. Why partial plans better capture optimizer behavior

An optimizer's decisions are not monolithic; they are compositions of many local decisions — a join method chosen for a particular pair of relations, a scan strategy chosen for a particular predicate, an aggregation strategy chosen for a particular grouping. Each of these local decisions is itself a legitimate, independently meaningful unit of optimizer behavior. A fragment-based decomposition treats every connected downward slice of the plan — not only full rooted subtrees — as a candidate unit of comparison, allowing the analysis to detect and credit agreement at whatever granularity it actually occurs, from a single operator up to the entire plan.

3. Fragment generation procedure

Starting from any node in the plan tree, a fragment is grown by making an independent ternary decision for every child of every included node: omit the child entirely, include the child as a leaf of the fragment (stopping the recursion there), or include the child and continue applying the same decision recursively to its own children. This process is applied exhaustively from every node in the tree, and the resulting fragments are canonicalized as ordered, labeled structures so that duplicate shapes arising from different generation paths collapse into a single counted instance. Traversal is strictly downward and unbounded in depth, so fragments range from single operators to arbitrarily deep, arbitrarily pruned connected slices of the plan.

4. Why Optimizer Coverage is an appropriate metric

Normalizing the count of shared unique fragments by the number of unique fragments in the original plan yields a directional, interpretable quantity: it answers "of everything the original optimizer expressed structurally in this plan, what fraction is also expressed by the synthetic plan?" Because the denominator is fixed to the original plan's own fragment vocabulary, the metric is not inflated by a synthetic plan that is simply larger or more complex — it is only credited for fragments that genuinely recur from the original, making it a conservative and behaviorally grounded measure of preservation.

Engineering note on exhaustive enumeration: fragment counts grow combinatorially with tree depth and branching. Across this workload, per-query unique fragment counts in the original and synthetic plans range from the low hundreds up to approximately 1.6 million for the deepest, most heavily branched plans. All coverage figures, fragment counts, and set intersections reported throughout this document are computed from the complete, exhaustively enumerated fragment sets with no sampling or approximation. For on-screen readability only, the raw fragment-text listings inside each query's expandable card are capped at 250 rows per category (common / original-only / synthetic-only); the counts and percentages themselves are exact and reflect the full sets.
